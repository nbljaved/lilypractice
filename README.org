#+TITLE: lilypractice

Piano practice from LilyPond scores, in the spirit of Sightread and
PianoBooster: the real engraving with a cursor that follows the music, three
practice modes, scoring, loops, and the hand you are not practising played
for you. Native (Go, GTK 4, librsvg, ALSA), for Guix.

*A vibe-coded project, for personal use.* An AI assistant (Claude) wrote
nearly all of it, as I asked for features and tried them out while learning
the piano. It does what I need on my machines (Linux, with Guix), and it is
tested (unit tests, end-to-end tests, 100 scores from Mutopia), but it is no
maintained product: expect rough edges and no support, and read the code
before you rely on it.

* What it does
- Shows a score as LilyPond engraves it, page by page, with a cursor that
  follows the music and notes lit as they sound: blue for the right hand,
  orange for the left.
- Opens any =.ly= file, and lists the others in its folder. Scores written
  for an older LilyPond (most of Mutopia's) are updated on a copy with
  =convert-ly=.
- Three modes: /wait/ (the music waits for your notes), /play/ (it keeps
  time and scores you: perfect, good, missed, wrong) and /listen/.
- Practise the right hand, the left or both; the program plays the rest.
- Tempo, loops of bars, a metronome with nine sounds, a count-in.
- Follows the score's repeats (volta repeats, alternative endings, D.S.
  and D.C. al Fine), tremolos, ties, grace notes, pedal markings and tempo
  changes; several scores in one file; hidden notes; transposing
  instruments.
- Plays with a MIDI keyboard, the computer keyboard (two rows like a
  piano) or the mouse on the piano on screen. Keys of a MIDI keyboard can
  be commands (play, back a bar, faster...).
- Sounds through FluidSynth, started for you, or any ALSA synth (Pianoteq,
  a hardware module...).

* What it doesn't do
- Read anything but LilyPond: no MusicXML, MIDI or PDF scores.
- Hear an acoustic piano: you play a MIDI keyboard, the computer keyboard
  or the mouse.
- Run outside Linux: it needs ALSA and GTK 4, and is only packaged for
  Guix.
- Edit or transpose scores, show falling notes (as Synthesia does), or
  keep a history of your practice across sessions.
- Follow repeats written only as text ("D.C. al Fine" in a markup, as older
  scores do) or as plain bar lines, or coda jumps; play percent repeats.
- See a tempo set only inside a =\midi= block (common in Mutopia files):
  such scores play at ♩ = 60. Use ↑ ↓ or =-tempo=.
- Play drum staves: unpitched notes are left out.

* Install and run
On my machines it is a Guix package, from the personal channel in my
dotfiles (=guix-config/modules/nbl/packages/lilypractice.scm=). From a
checkout, =run.sh= builds and starts it instead:
#+begin_src sh
./run.sh testdata/bach-invention-01.ly   # this piece; the list has its folder
./run.sh ~/music                         # choose from the scores of ~/music
./run.sh                                 # ... of the current folder
./run.sh -mode play -hand left -tempo 70 piece.ly
./run.sh -h          # all flags
#+end_src
Installed, the command is =lilypractice=, with the same arguments. =run.sh=
builds the program in the Guix environment from =manifest.scm= (the first
build takes about 5 minutes) and starts it. It drops =LD_LIBRARY_PATH=: on
this machine that points at Nix's alsa-lib, which breaks Guix programs that
use ALSA.

A list on the left shows the LilyPond files (=.ly=) of the folder, with the
title and composer from their =\header=; click one to practise it. It
keeps up as files are added or removed. F9, or the button at the top left,
shows or hides it; drag its edge to make it wider.

A score is compiled with LilyPond the first time, in the background (the
window says so, and you can choose another meanwhile), and reused while
the =.ly= file is unchanged. Click the score's name again after editing it
to load it afresh; =-recompile= always runs LilyPond (needed after editing
only a file the score =\include=s). Mode, hand, tempo and the other
settings stay as they were when you change scores; the loop doesn't.

* Scores from the web
Most =.ly= files on the web, Mutopia's among them, were written for an
older LilyPond. lilypractice copies such a score (and the files it
=\include=s) into its cache, updates the copy with =convert-ly=, and
compiles that; your file is left alone. If LilyPond reports errors but
still makes the score (an unknown layout setting, say), it is shown anyway
and the errors are printed.

- *Repeats* are followed: volta repeats go back as often as they say and
  take their first, second... endings in turn, and =\repeat segno= gives
  D.S. or D.C. al Fine. The transport shows "2nd time" and so on. Settings →
  Follow repeats turns this off; a loop always plays its bars as written.
  A tie into a second ending doesn't hold the note over the jump.
- *Tremolos* are played as the strikes they stand for: =\repeat tremolo 4
  c8= (and =c2:8=) as four eighths, =\repeat tremolo 4 { c16 e }= as c and e
  in turn.
- *Several scores in one file* (movements, or pieces of a set): the first
  is practised; =-score N= or the list in the header picks another. A
  =\score= without staves (a chord drawn in a markup) doesn't count.
- *Hidden notes* (transparent, =\hideNotes=) sound but are always played for
  you, as you can't read them.
- *Transposing instruments* (a horn in F above the piano) sound at their
  real pitch.
- *Which staff is which hand*: a piano's own two staves (a =PianoStaff= or
  =GrandStaff=) come first, so a singer's or a horn's staff above them is
  played for you; then staves named for each hand (=right=, =lh=, =upper=,
  =pianoDown=, =links=...); then the top two.

* Modes
- *Wait*: the music stops at each of your notes until you play them, then
  the other hand comes in.
- *Play*: the music keeps time, as with a real accompanist, and scores your
  notes: /Perfect/ within 50 ms, /Good/ within 300 ms, /Missed/ if not
  played in time, /Wrong/ for a key that wasn't due. Turn on the *guide* to
  hear your part quietly while you learn it.
- *Listen*: the program plays the music: both hands, or only the right or
  the left, to hear one part alone (staves that are neither hand's always
  play). =-mode listen= starts with both hands.

Choose which hand you play (right, left or both), the tempo, a *loop* of
bars, the metronome and a count-in (0–4 bars, in the settings; the cursor
waits at the start while it counts down, and keys you try meanwhile don't
count). The metronome and count-in can
sound as a wood block, a metronome (bell and click), claves, side stick,
cowbell, hi-hat, triangle, hand clap or sticks, at 10–140% volume: in the
settings, or right-click the Metronome button. You hear the new sound at
once. A hand is greyed out when the
score has none of its notes on your keyboard (in listen mode, none at
all), as Left and Both are for a piece on one staff. The stats (accuracy, streak, perfect / good / missed /
wrong) are in the header; missed notes stay red on the score until the
next pass.

*Looping*: drag across bars on the timeline or on the score to loop them;
drag the loop's edges on the timeline to change it. The looped bars are
shaded on the score. The Loop button (F6) turns it on and off.

*Pedals*: the score's pedal markings (=\sustainOn= / =\sustainOff=, and
sostenuto and una corda) are played with the music. Your own sustain pedal
— a MIDI keyboard's, or *Shift* on the computer keyboard — keeps your notes
ringing; the Pedal light in the transport bar shows when it is down.

*Your keyboard*: in the settings, choose its size (88, 76, 61, 49, 37 or 25
keys), or press /Detect/ and then its lowest and highest key. The piano
shows those keys, and in wait and play mode, notes outside them are played
for you. The size, the computer keys' octave, the count-in, and the view
settings are kept in =~/.config/lilypractice/prefs.json=.

* The window
The header (a button for the list of scores, title, stats, keys,
settings); on the left the list of scores; then, top to bottom, the score
with its scroll bars, a piano, a timeline of the bars, and the transport
bar (restart, play, bar, pedal, mode, hands, tempo, loop, metronome).

- Notes are coloured by hand, on the score and the piano: blue for the
  right hand, orange for the left, violet for any other staff; green is
  what you just played, red what you missed. The cursor flashes red on a
  wrong key. A note tied on from another (joined to it by a curve) isn't
  played again but held; it lights up as the music reaches it.
- The piano has a real piano's proportions: its height sets the key size,
  and it is centred (drag the line above it to resize it). A dot marks the
  key to play. When that note is beyond the computer keys, a ring marks the
  same note on them, as any octave counts; the rings stop once you play a
  MIDI keyboard.
- The view follows the music smoothly, pausing for a few seconds after you
  scroll. In the settings: one page or two side by side (F8), the cursor's
  style (a column over the staves, a line, the bar, or only the notes) and
  colour, the piano's height and the zoom.

The mouse:

| Where       | Does                                                          |
|-------------+---------------------------------------------------------------|
| score       | click a note: start there; double-click: play from there      |
|             | right-click: play from here, start here, loop this bar / up   |
|             | to here / four bars, loop off, fit to width, one or two pages |
|             | drag over bars: loop them; shift-click: loop to that bar      |
|             | drag empty space, or middle-drag anywhere: move the view      |

| timeline    | click: start at that bar; double-click: play from it          |

* Playing
- *Computer keyboard*: two rows like a piano, by key position, so any layout
  works.
  #+begin_example
   s d   g h j   l ;          2 3   5 6 7   9 0   =
  z x c v b n m , . /        q w e r t y u i o p [ ]
  #+end_example
  The bottom row starts at C3 and the top row an octave higher; PgUp/PgDn
  move both by an octave. By default a computer key counts in any octave
  (F4 switches to exact octaves), since two rows cannot reach the whole
  piano.
- *MIDI keyboard*: connect it to =lilypractice:keyboard-in=, in qpwgraph or
  with =-in "SL88"=. =-channel N= listens to one MIDI channel only.
- *Simulated keyboard*: =out/bin/lpsim piece.ly= plays the right hand into
  =lilypractice:0= at the written tempo; =-hand=, =-tempo=, =-wrong 0.1= (some
  mistakes), =-delay=, =-to CLIENT:0=. Or play any MIDI file into it:
  =aplaymidi -p lilypractice:0 file.mid=.

* MIDI command keys
Some keys of your MIDI keyboard can be commands: play, back a bar, faster,
and so on. Give them a zone of their own on MIDI channel 16 (on the SL88,
e.g. A0–E1); notes on that channel then never sound or count as notes.
lilypractice writes this default to =~/.config/lilypractice/commands.json=:

| Key   | Does            | Key   | Does              |
|-------+-----------------+-------+-------------------|
| A0    | back a bar      | A♯0   | next mode         |
| B0    | play / stop     | C♯1   | next hand         |
| C1    | forward a bar   | D♯1   | metronome         |
| D1    | slower          | A0+C1 | back to the start |
| E1    | faster          | D1+E1 | loop on / off     |

#+begin_src json
{
  "Channel": 16,
  "Keys": {
    "A0": "bar-back",
    "A0+C1": "start",
    ...
  }
}
#+end_src

- Keys are written =A0=, =C#1= (or =C♯1=), =Bb0= (or =B♭0=), or as MIDI note
  numbers; keys held together are joined by =+=. A key that is part of a
  combination acts when you let it go, so that holding it for the
  combination doesn't also run it; other keys act when they go down.
- =Channel= 0 means any channel: then only the keys named are commands, and
  the rest play. That suits a keyboard without zones, giving up a few keys.
- Edit the file while lilypractice runs: it reads it again at once. The
  keys panel (keyboard icon in the header) lists the keys and any problem
  with the file.
- Commands: =play-stop=, =start=, =bar-back=, =bar-forward=, =note-back=,
  =note-forward=, =slower=, =faster=, =metronome=, =count-in=, =loop=,
  =guide=, =next-mode=, =wait-mode=, =play-mode=, =listen-mode=,
  =next-hand=, =right-hand=, =left-hand=, =both-hands=, =pages=.

*With midi-keys and xremap* (MIDI → uinput → xremap): lilypractice reads
the zone itself, so keep xremap's MIDI-key rules away from it, or each key
does two things (B0 would both play and, through xremap, press Up:
faster). Add this to each =midi-keys= entry in =xremap.yaml= (its window
class is =lilypractice=):
#+begin_src yaml
    application:
      not: [lilypractice]
#+end_src
Or the other way round: set =Keys= to ={}= and let xremap send
lilypractice's computer keys (above); every command has one.

* Sound
lilypractice is an ALSA client (named =lilypractice=, or =-name NAME= to
run several) with four ports, which also show in qpwgraph:

| Port | Name        | What                                                     |
|------+-------------+----------------------------------------------------------|
|    0 | keyboard-in | your MIDI keyboard                                       |
|    1 | score-out   | notes and pedals it plays from the score; channel = staff |
|    2 | click-out   | metronome and count-in (channel 10, wood blocks)         |
|    3 | keys-out    | notes from the computer keyboard, so you hear them        |

- =-synth auto= (default) starts FluidSynth with the FluidR3 soundfont and
  connects ports 1–3 to it. It stops when lilypractice stops. =-audio= picks
  FluidSynth's audio driver (=pipewire=, =jack=, =alsa=).
- =-synth Pianoteq= (any ALSA client name) connects to that instead.
- =-synth none= leaves the ports for you to wire, e.g. to Carla, Ardour or
  SuperCollider.

A MIDI keyboard should go straight to the instrument too, not through
lilypractice, for the lowest latency; =-thru= passes keyboard-in on to
keys-out for setups where it can't.

* Other commands
- =out/bin/lpfetch [-n 100] [-per 3]= :: download keyboard pieces from Mutopia
  into =testdata/mutopia= (the listing pages are kept, so a second run
  fetches only what is missing).
- =out/bin/lpcheck piece.ly...= :: compile and check that the SVG note
  heads, the JSON note list and LilyPond's own MIDI output agree.
- =out/bin/lprender -o page.png piece.ly 0 5= :: write a page as PNG with
  the notes of onsets 0 and 5 coloured.

* Tests
#+begin_src sh
env -u LD_LIBRARY_PATH guix shell -m manifest.scm -- go test ./...
test/e2e.sh      # the real program on a virtual display, without sound
test/corpus.sh   # 100 real scores from Mutopia (about 3 minutes)
#+end_src
The engine's tests engrave their pieces in =testdata= with LilyPond (kept in
the temporary folder until they change). The end-to-end tests use their own
ALSA client name, so a lilypractice you have open is not disturbed.

*Corpus*: =out/bin/lpfetch= downloaded 100 keyboard pieces from Mutopia into
=testdata/mutopia= (at most three per composer, picked at random with a
fixed seed; =index.tsv= lists their titles, composers, licences and
sources). =test/corpus.sh= runs =lpcheck= on all of them: each must compile,
its notes must match its note heads and LilyPond's own MIDI (as written,
or with repeats and tremolos played out), Listen mode must play every note
in order, a perfect player in Wait mode must get through without a
wrong or missed note, and every note head must be on a line of music with
staves (which the cursor spans). 85 pass; =testdata/mutopia/known.tsv= says
why each of the other 15 doesn't (scores LilyPond 2.26 can't compile even after
=convert-ly=, or MIDI parts written differently from the printed music).
Anything else is reported as NEW.

* How it works
=lilypond/lilypractice.ly= is loaded into LilyPond with
=-dinclude-settings=, so scores need no changes. It gives each note head an
SVG id and writes =piece.notes.json= with every note's time, length, pitch,
staff, voice and tremolo, plus ties, bars, tempos, time signatures, pedals
and repeats. The program plays from that JSON and finds each note in the
picture by its id.

| Directory   | What it holds                                            |
|-------------+----------------------------------------------------------|
| =lilypond/= | the LilyPond plugin, and the code that runs LilyPond     |
| =score/=    | reads the JSON note list; repeats, tremolos              |
| =practice/= | the practice engine: timing, modes, scoring, loop        |
| =midikeys/= | MIDI keys as commands: single keys and combinations      |
| =alsa/=     | small ALSA sequencer binding                             |
| =synth/=    | starts FluidSynth                                        |
| =rsvg/=     | small librsvg binding: draw, find and recolour notes     |
| =view/=     | draws the stacked pages with coloured notes              |
| =cmd/=      | lilypractice and the helper commands                     |
| =test/=     | end-to-end tests                                         |
| =testdata/= | test pieces                                              |
